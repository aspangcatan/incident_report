Investigation Objective

Determine operational systemic vulnerabilities leading to double-rate administration of Norepinephrine infusion (0.2 mcg/kg/min vs programmed 0.4 mcg/kg/min) during the 22:00 shift handover in Emergency Bay 3, evaluate pump hardware logs, and establish failsafe verification barriers.

Root Cause Analysis Workbench

Adverse Outcome / Direct Trigger

Secondary Norepinephrine infusion delivered at 2x intended clinical rate during 22:45 handover transition.

1
Why was the secondary infusion rate doubled?

The Alaris infusion pump unit (#P-084) was manually set using an incorrect patient weight metric (92 kg entered vs 46 kg true weight).

2
Why was an incorrect weight entered into the pump?

Patient bedside records were swapped when Beds 04-A and 04-B were re-allocated to accommodate an incoming acute polytrauma case during ER influx.

3
Why were charts swapped without immediate correction?

Staff utilized non-standardized temporary clipboards and adhesive notes during bedside triage due to local workstation terminal downtime.

4
Why were emergency workstation terminals unavailable?

Hospital IT was executing an uncoordinated database index rebuild without an operational bypass terminal or high-availability failover active.

5
Primary Root Cause Identified Ref: RCA-2026-F5

Absence of an enforced Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) requiring physical 2-RN barcode verification and independent dual check during hospital electronic downtime situations.

Contributory factor: Lack of programmed Dose Error Reduction System (DERS) hard-limits on pump hardware firmware v2.14.

Triangulated Contributing Factors

volume_up Environmental & Ergonomic

Ambient noise measured at 84dB in ER Bay 3 during acute resuscitation, compounding auditory fatigue. Overhead lighting glare masked LCD decimal dot.

devices Systemic & Technological

Standalone infusion units operated unlinked to the central hospital pharmacy dispensing system, preventing remote automated rate alerts.
